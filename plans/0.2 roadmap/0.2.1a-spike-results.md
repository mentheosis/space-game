## Manual Review Round 1

Reported findings:

- The cockpit and overall shuttle scale look too small compared to the player height.
- The first red proxy boxes did not match the ship well, especially the swept-back wings.
- The player could not stand on the wings or red proxy boxes and fell through to the interior or ground.
- Hatch entry, sitting, and ship flight still worked.

Interpretation:

- ShuttleA is still a good visual direction, but the current scale likely needs adjustment before serious collision fitting.
- Rectangular primitives can be rotated and combined, but square boxes alone will not be enough for the wing shape.
- Triangular or custom collision-only meshes should be considered for swept surfaces if rotated boxes are not good enough.
- The first fall-through result likely came from spike collision shapes being nested under a plain `Node3D` instead of attached directly to the ship physics body.

## Manual Review Round 2

Reported findings:

- Standing on the ship mostly feels stable.
- The visual wings sweep farther back than the red proxy boxes.
- The red boxes are generally shorter than the rounded center mass of the visual model, causing clipping through upper ship geometry.
- The visual model has a vertical tail fin that needs collision.
- Some interior model/collision still clips through the exterior visual model and interferes with exterior traversal.
- Hatch entry, sitting, and ship flight still work.

Interpretation:

- Landed-ship standing is viable enough to continue.
- Compound primitives can work, but they need to be more numerous, rotated, and layered to approximate the ship's rounded/swept silhouette.
- The wing collision probably needs either longer rotated boxes or collision-only triangular/convex meshes.
- The tail fin needs an explicit blocking shape.
- Interior/exterior separation is a real requirement for 0.2.1b; the current interior shell cannot remain blindly mixed with exterior collision.

Changes made after Round 2:

- Swept the wing proxies farther back.
- Increased wing proxy height.
- Increased central fuselage proxy height.
- Raised the top deck proxy.
- Added an upper-hull proxy to approximate the rounded center mass.
- Added a vertical tail-fin proxy.

Open question:

- The wing shape is swept/triangular. Even measured boxes still include empty space. If this pass remains visually wrong, the next experiment should use collision-only convex wing meshes rather than more boxes.

## Manual Review Round 3

Reported findings:

- Mesh-derived red boxes are better aligned than the previous hand-placed proxies.
- They still include too much empty space, especially around swept wing regions.
- Standing on top of the ship is stable.

Interpretation:

- Landed ship exterior traversal is viable.
- Compound primitive boxes are stable enough for broad hull and simple massing.
- Axis-aligned or lightly rotated boxes are not sufficient for the swept wings if we want the collision to feel faithful to the visible model.
- The 0.2.1c ship collision pass should use a hybrid approach:
  - compound primitives for central hull, underside, broad top hull, and simple blocking masses,
  - collision-only convex pieces or low-poly collision meshes for swept wings and tail/fin areas.

## Spike Decision Record

### Ship Model

Decision: keep the current OpenGameArt `ShuttleA.obj` as the 0.2 ship model.

Reason:

- The model is visually good enough to integrate.
- The overall dimensions are large enough for a ship.
- The cockpit/canopy still looks somewhat small relative to the standing player, so 0.2.1b should address physical scale/interior alignment before 0.2.1c fits final exterior collision.

### Ship Scale

Decision: add a dedicated 0.2.1b phase for ship scale and physical interior/exterior alignment before the serious exterior collision pass.

Reason:

- The ship is usable for exterior collision and traversal experiments, but the cockpit/interior relationship looks too small relative to the player.
- Scaling should not be changed casually because it affects hatch markers, ship camera, collision, landing footprint, and flight feel.
- The next phase should decide and apply any scale/interior-envelope adjustment before exterior collision is fitted more carefully.
- Final cockpit art, transparent glass, and hatch animation can remain in 0.2.2, but physical scale and temporary interior alignment need to happen in 0.2.1.

### Collision Strategy

Decision: use a hybrid collision strategy for 0.2.1c.

Recommended:

- Use compound primitives for:
  - central fuselage,
  - underside,
  - broad top hull,
  - basic blocking masses.
- Use collision-only convex meshes or low-poly collision pieces for:
  - swept wings,
  - tail fin,
  - other triangular or strongly tapered forms.

Avoid:

- Raw render mesh collision for the dynamic ship body.
- More large rectangular boxes around swept wings, because they include too much empty space.

### Physics Stability

Decision: landed/frozen ship standing is viable enough to continue.

Reason:

- Standing on top of the spike collision is reported as stable.
- The next phase can focus on fidelity and traversal routes rather than first proving whether standing is possible.

### Main Risks For 0.2.1b And 0.2.1c

- Interior shell and exterior collision currently interfere with each other.
- Cockpit/interior scale may require ship visual scale or temporary interior layout changes before exterior collision is finalized.
- Swept wings require custom collision pieces, not simple boxes.
- Collision must remain compatible with hatch, seat, takeoff, landing, and flight.

### 0.2.1c Collision Implications

The serious exterior collision pass should now fit collision against the larger visual scale and avoid assuming the old rectangular interior shell.

Recommended 0.2.1c direction remains:

- broad primitives for central fuselage and stable walkable top surfaces,
- collision-only convex or low-poly pieces for swept wings and tail,
- careful separation between interior collision, exterior traversal collision, and hatch interaction volumes.

### Recommended Acceptance Criteria For 0.2.1c

- Player can stand on the landed ship hull for at least 5 seconds without falling through or jittering badly.
- Player can traverse a hatch-area to top-hull route.
- Player can jump from hull to ground without launch/stuck bugs.
- Wing collision does not contain large obvious empty-space blockers.
- Tail/fin collision blocks the visible fin region without excessive invisible walls.
- Interior collision does not interfere with exterior traversal.
- Hatches, seat, takeoff, landing, stand, and exit still work.
