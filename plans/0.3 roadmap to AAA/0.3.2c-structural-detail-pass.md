# 0.3.2c Structural Detail Pass

## Objective

Turn the approved prototype shuttle blockout into a more believable authored ship interior/exterior structure without changing the accepted silhouette, traversal route, or collision foundation.

This pass is primarily about geometry quality and structural coherence. Materials and lighting may receive basic support work, but the full material/lighting pass remains the next phase.

## Goal Statement

Create the first production-quality structural art pass for the prototype shuttle interior, with the ramp, hatch frame, cargo deck, ascent route, and cockpit entry reading as one coherent manufactured spacecraft interior.

Use the reference images for design language, not as literal copies:

- `cargo_ramp_ref.jpg` should guide the ramp opening, hatch frame thickness, cargo bay sidewall rhythm, ceiling light housings, floor paneling, and clear central entry route.
- `interior_lighting_material_ref.jpeg` should guide manufactured panel discipline, material-family separation, floor/wall trim, and practical fixture placement.
- `cockpit_ref_1.webp`, `cockpit_ref_2.png`, and `cockpit_ref_3.webp` should guide cockpit frame clarity, seat scale, transparent canopy readability, and avoidance of cockpit clutter.

By the end of this pass, the ship should no longer read as a playable gray/transparent blockout. It should read as an intentional early vertical-slice ship interior with believable structural parts, even if final materials, UVs, texture polish, and lighting tuning are still pending.

## Execution Process

Work in large, coherent passes instead of tiny isolated edits. Each pass should make a visible improvement to a whole region and should be reviewed against the references before export.

### Step 1: Preserve And Inspect Source

Before editing:

- confirm `assets/source/blender/ships/prototype_shuttle/prototype_shuttle.blend` is the active source
- do not run procedural bootstrap
- inspect the ramp doorway cut and ensure it remains intact
- inspect current player route from ramp to cargo deck to cockpit
- keep a copy of the current source/evidence state available for comparison if needed

Acceptance:

- the authored doorway cut remains in the Blender source
- the current traversal route is understood before adding detail

### Step 2: Reference Breakdown

Before modeling each region, translate the references into concrete geometry targets.

For the ramp/cargo reference, extract:

- hatch frame: thick side jambs, upper frame/lintel, lower threshold/hinge mass
- ramp: side rails, inset treads, panel seams, hinge line, edge trims
- cargo bay: sidewall panel modules, vertical supports, ceiling ribs, overhead light housings, central floor panels
- composition: clear sightline from ramp into ship, readable walking path, side detail kept out of the route

For cockpit references, extract:

- canopy frame/gasket structure
- low-profile pilot/copilot seats
- console/control envelope that does not block the canopy
- clean sightline forward and upward

Acceptance:

- every modeled detail has an identifiable reference reason or ship-function reason
- no new detail is added merely as random decoration

### Step 3: Region Pass Order

Implement in this order:

1. **Ramp And Hatch Frame**
   Build the opening first because it defines the transition from exterior to interior.

2. **Cargo Deck Shell**
   Add floor, sidewall, and ceiling structure around the central walking path.

3. **Cargo-To-Cockpit Ascent**
   Replace temporary stair/ascent shapes with believable structure and support.

4. **Cockpit Structural Cleanup**
   Improve seats, canopy frame relation, floor perimeter, and cockpit entry.

5. **Exterior Hatch/Underside Support**
   Add exterior trim and fixtures only after the ramp/cargo relationship is clear.

Acceptance:

- each region can be reviewed independently
- no pass breaks earlier accepted traversal

### Step 4: Model Authoring Rules

Use authored Blender geometry in the correct collections.

Required modeling behavior:

- keep primary walkable surfaces clear
- use bevels/rounded edges where parts would be manufactured or padded
- keep panels aligned to hull/cargo bay axes
- prefer reusable module patterns over one-off clutter
- create visible light housings even if final light tuning comes later
- use simple material placeholders to distinguish hull, floor, trim, rubber, glass, emissive housings, and seat fabric
- keep collision unchanged unless a visible structure requires a matching adjustment

Avoid:

- free-floating panels
- paper-thin structural ribs
- oversized opaque cockpit objects
- details crossing the player path
- broad invisible collision changes
- deleting or moving the manual ramp doorway cut

### Step 5: Validation And Evidence Loop

After each meaningful region pass:

1. Export the authored source.
2. Validate package and collision layout.
3. Import in Godot.
4. Capture evidence.
5. Review the evidence against the reference-derived targets.
6. If geometry affects traversal, run the layout scene manually.

Do not mark a region complete if the evidence still shows:

- missing wall/floor/ceiling structure
- ramp/hatch frame that looks like loose geometry
- detail blocking the central route
- cockpit seats or consoles blocking forward view
- visual/collision disagreement visible to the player

### Step 6: Completion Review

The phase is complete only when:

- the ramp/hatch area reads as a real opening mechanism
- the lower cargo deck reads as enclosed and manufactured
- the cargo-to-cockpit ascent reads as built into the ship
- the cockpit entry and seats are clearer and human-scaled
- the central route is visually obvious and traversable
- evidence captures show the improvements clearly
- validation passes without relying on procedural regeneration

## Production Rules

- Preserve the accepted exterior silhouette.
- Preserve the manually cut ramp doorway.

## Scope

### 1. Ramp And Hatch Frame

Tasks:

- thicken the ramp doorway frame so it reads as a real hull opening
- add side jambs, upper lintel/threshold, and lower hinge/threshold geometry
- add ramp side rails or edge trim
- add ramp surface paneling and inset tread strips
- add hinge/mechanical hints at the ramp top edge
- add exterior underside light fixtures near the ramp, but keep final light tuning for the lighting pass

Acceptance:

- the ramp no longer reads as a loose plank
- the doorway cut looks intentional from exterior and interior views
- the player can visually understand where the hatch would close later
- ramp detail does not block traversal

### 2. Cargo Deck Structure

Tasks:

- replace broad flat cargo sidewalls with panelized wall sections
- add ceiling ribs or overhead structure aligned with the hull
- add ceiling light housings as visible geometry
- add floor panel seams, central walking path panels, and side inset panels
- add side equipment recesses, vents, grab rails, tie-downs, or small storage panels
- keep the lower deck enclosed and visually connected to the exterior hull

Acceptance:

- cargo bay reads as a manufactured interior space rather than a translucent volume proxy
- floor, walls, and ceiling are complete and coherent
- the central route from ramp inward is immediately legible
- wall/ceiling details do not produce visual clutter or collision snags

### 3. Cargo-To-Cockpit Ascent

Tasks:

- improve the side stairs/ascent from blockout boxes into believable stepped structure
- add side rail, trim, or support geometry where appropriate
- ensure the ascent does not block the ramp entry
- visually connect ascent geometry to cargo floor and cockpit floor

Acceptance:

- the player can see how to reach the cockpit deck
- stairs/side ascent look intentionally built into the ship
- traversal remains valid from ramp to cockpit

### 4. Cockpit Structural Cleanup

Tasks:

- refine cockpit floor perimeter and deck transition
- improve pilot/copilot seat blockout enough that they read as seats, not simple boxes
- add basic cockpit frame/gasket support around the canopy
- add console/control envelope without blocking pilot view
- keep forward sightline clear through the canopy

Acceptance:

- cockpit structure aligns with the exterior canopy and neck volume
- pilot/copilot seats are human-scaled and do not dominate the view
- no major opaque structure blocks the intended forward view

### 5. Exterior Structural Hints

Tasks:

- add or refine belly hatch/ramp exterior trim
- add simple landing/underside support detail where it helps the ship read from exterior views
- add engine/wing/fuselage trim only where it supports the current silhouette

Acceptance:

- exterior still matches the accepted silhouette
- added detail reinforces function rather than changing the ship category
- ramp/hatch location is visible and believable from outside

## Out Of Scope

- final PBR texture authoring
- final lighting tuning
- animated ramp/hatch
- full exterior walkable collision
- major cockpit redesign
- changing the ship scale or gross silhouette
- procedural regeneration of the source scene
