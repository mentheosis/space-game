# Space Hope To Achieve Iteration 7

Iteration 7 should improve perceived depth across the interior, especially ceiling and upper wall surfaces. The current interior has many panels, but too many are still flat layers rather than believable built structure.

## 1. Material Families

I want upper wall and ceiling surfaces to separate into darker structural ribs, lighter removable liners, brushed metal cable trays, rubber gaskets, and emissive diffuser lenses.

## 2. Practical Lighting

I want ceiling and side light sources to look more directional and physically mounted. This pass should add visible housings/backers and tune the review lights so pools land on the cargo floor, side stairs, and cockpit threshold.

## 3. Ramp/Cargo Polish

I want the cargo bay to gain more believable overhead utility depth: cable trays, service channels, rib caps, and larger removable ceiling panels, while preserving the walkable route.

## 4. Cockpit Readability

I want cockpit overhead and side canopy structures to feel connected to the cargo ceiling language. Add restrained overhead detail and light-source mounts that help frame the seats without obstructing the view.

## 5. Evidence Update

I want the moving walkthrough to better reveal ceiling depth during the upward sweep. The evidence should show that looking up reveals built structure, not just dark/flat ceiling planes.

## Success Assessment Before Iteration 8

Iteration 7 was successful enough to move on.

The material family target improved through clearer ceiling liner, rib cap, dark channel, brushed tray, and diffuser separation.

The practical lighting target improved: overhead fixtures look more mounted and directional.

The ramp/cargo polish target improved with more overhead utility depth while keeping traversal surfaces stable.

The cockpit readability target improved modestly because overhead cockpit fixtures and liner details now connect the cockpit to the cargo ceiling language.

The evidence target succeeded: upward sweep frames show more built structure than before. Iteration 8 should now focus on manufacturing detail and surface breakup so large surfaces stop reading as plain panels.
