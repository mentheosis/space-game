# Space Hope To Achieve Iteration 5

Iteration 5 should attack the dominant cockpit readability defect: too much of the pilot area reads as large black masses in motion. The goal is to preserve the dark sci-fi cockpit mood while making the dashboard, seats, canopy sill, and route edges readable.

## 1. Material Families

I want the cockpit to use more mid-tone metal and fabric surfaces around the dashboard and seats. This pass should reduce overuse of nearly black trim in the pilot station and replace it with brushed metal, worn control surfaces, fabric panels, rubber gaskets, and smoked glass in more deliberate zones.

## 2. Practical Lighting

I want the cockpit lights to reveal form without flattening the space. This pass should add small visible instrument strips and side glows that separate the dashboard top, seat backs, armrests, and footwell from the canopy darkness.

## 3. Ramp/Cargo Polish

I want the ramp/cargo area to remain stable, with only small targeted additions that help continuity: labels, guide marks, and threshold details that do not compete with the cockpit.

## 4. Cockpit Readability

I want the two-seat cockpit to become legible from side and oblique sweep frames. This pass should shrink or visually break up the large black dashboard/seat silhouettes, add lighter facing panels where appropriate, and make control surfaces read as low and forward rather than as a wall.

## 5. Evidence Update

I want the moving walkthrough and static cockpit views to prove whether the large black-mass problem is fixed. The cockpit sweep should keep showing oblique side views, because those are currently revealing the real defect.

## Success Assessment Before Iteration 6

Iteration 5 made only modest progress.

The material family target improved slightly: seat fabric and console materials are less uniformly black, and the dashboard has more pale, brushed, and emissive breakup.

The practical lighting target improved slightly with dashboard and seat-separation light, but the moving walkthrough still shows large dark side/canopy areas during the cockpit sweep.

The ramp/cargo polish target remained stable and gained small threshold labels.

The cockpit readability target remains unresolved. The important lesson is that the required 90-degree cockpit sweep looks into side/canopy areas that are still under-modeled and under-lit. Breaking up the forward console alone does not fix the sweep.

The evidence target succeeded as diagnosis: it proved the next pass should address side-cockpit surfaces and lighting directly instead of continuing to tweak the same forward dashboard pieces.
