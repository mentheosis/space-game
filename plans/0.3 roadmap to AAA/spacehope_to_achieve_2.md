# Space Hope To Achieve Iteration 2

Iteration 2 should use the first walkthrough result to make the interior feel less like a lit blockout and more like a worn operational shuttle bay. The most important lesson from iteration 1 is that material separation and practical lights exist, but the lighting and detail still do not consistently explain the form of the space.

## 1. Material Families

I want the next render to show a richer mid-tone range so that dark metal, worn metal, floor inserts, pale panels, rubber, and seat fabric separate without relying only on color. This iteration should add a second family of scratched edge metal and more deliberate use of worn inserts. This is ambitious because it should change the perceived surface quality across most visible routes instead of adding isolated decorative spots.

## 2. Practical Lighting

I want the walkthrough to read the stairs and cockpit approach without broad debug wash. The next render should add visible low side lights and stair/cockpit practicals that throw directionally useful pools onto steps, walls, and the cockpit floor. This is ambitious because it addresses the main weakness from iteration 1: the interior was moody, but too many important surfaces were not shaped by visible light.

## 3. Ramp/Cargo Polish

I want the ramp and cargo bay to gain mechanical credibility: hinge cylinders, cable-chain logic, wall service boxes, retainer rails, tie-down tracks, and cargo floor wear should make the bay feel used and functional. This is ambitious because it should make the cargo area read as a real transport compartment rather than a corridor with decorative panels.

## 4. Cockpit Readability

I want the cockpit to show a more intentional pilot station: clearer console surfaces, hand controls, MFD-like glowing panels, seat harnesses, and canopy sill structure. The chair silhouettes should remain readable from the sweep, and the pilot view should not be visually blocked. This is ambitious because it improves the highest-value review area while preserving the current traversal and two-seat layout.

## 5. Evidence Update

I want the evidence to make iteration 2 harder to fool. The moving walkthrough should remain, but the static material capture set should include clearer named views for ramp close-up, cargo aisle, top landing, and cockpit sweep support. This is ambitious because better evidence will force the next decisions to be based on problem areas instead of only favorable angles.

## Success Assessment Before Iteration 3

Iteration 2 was successful as a broad structural/material pass, but only partially successful as a moving-review pass.

The material family target improved: the generated shuttle now has scratched edge metal, sooty recesses, stenciled labels, brighter emissive fixtures, more deliberate floor wear, and more separation between rubber, panels, worn metal, and seat fabric.

The practical lighting target improved in static evidence: low wall lights, stair markers, cockpit overhead lights, and instrument glow are visible and explain more of the interior. In the moving walkthrough, however, several sweep frames still become too dark or point into exterior/canopy voids, so the video does not consistently prove material quality.

The ramp/cargo polish target improved substantially: hinge bars, brackets, service conduits, cable clamps, scuffs, tie-down tracks, low wall fixtures, and equipment details make the ramp and cargo bay read more like functional machinery.

The cockpit readability target improved but remains weaker than the cargo area. The seats, controls, belts, MFD bezels, side consoles, and sill conduits are clearer, but the cockpit still needs stronger silhouette control and better review lighting so that the pilot station does not collapse into black shapes from some angles.

The evidence target improved for static captures through better labels and review angles. The moving walkthrough remains the next bottleneck: it needs a route and light setup that keeps useful interior surfaces visible while still satisfying the required left/right/up sweeps.
